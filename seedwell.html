The problem

Financial education without the follow-through

Seedwell's premise was that financial education alone was not enough. The product focused on turning financial intentions into actions through payroll-based automation and connected accounts.

That meant designing one system across two audiences: a member app tied to payroll, and an employer portal that made a benefits team confident in the program they had bought. Both sat on compliance-sensitive ground, which shaped the product as much as the design did.

The Seedwell member app

Automation, not advice

Seedwell used payroll and connected financial accounts to help members organize everyday money movement. The design challenge was making automation feel understandable, controllable and trustworthy rather than invisible or intimidating.
Financial overview — accounts, balances, allocations and progress in one place.
Automated Income Allocation — how incoming money is directed across financial priorities.
Banking — transfers, bill payment and connected accounts in one place.
Priorities — goals ordered by importance, with progress against each.

LegacyAI

Taking the model directly to consumers

The Seedwell team later developed LegacyAI as a separately branded B2C extension for people outside the employer-benefits channel. I carried the product, brand and front-end thinking into a direct-to-consumer journey while adapting the experience for users who no longer entered through an employer program.

The product retained financial-management concepts such as account and payroll connections, automated money allocation and goal-based structures, while reframing onboarding, positioning and the overall user journey for direct consumer access.

The allocation engine gave the product its name. Rather than asking someone to budget, it reads the available balance against their goals and connected accounts, then proposes a split across spending, saving, investing and debt repayment. One action commits it: the money moves to the right accounts, and each run is recorded so the reasoning stays inspectable rather than opaque.

Direct onboarding — the user sets up alone, with no employer program to enter through.
Connections — payroll linked by the user, with permissions stated plainly.
Legacy AI — one action reads the balance and splits it across goals.
Allocation history — every run opens into its split, destination accounts and fees.

Design system

One system across three product surfaces

The Seedwell member app, employer portal and internal tools shared a common component system and front-end architecture. For a small team, reuse was essential: it kept the experience consistent, reduced implementation overhead and gave developers established patterns to build from.

Balances, transactions, allocation controls, goals and progress states

Front-end engineering

Designing the system meant building it too

I designed and implemented most of the user-facing front end across Seedwell and LegacyAI, translating shared Figma patterns into reusable production interfaces while collaborating with backend engineering on financial data, integrations and compliance-sensitive requirements.

Leadership & traction

Building the product while growing the team

As Co-Founder & Head of Engineering I mentored up to two interns and junior-to-intermediate developers at a time, coordinated implementation priorities and sprint planning, and worked alongside backend, financial-services, sales, partnerships and compliance stakeholders.

Seedwell moved through product validation into late-stage pre-launch development, producing a relatively complete product before operations ended. The employer and benefits-partner pipeline included 14 partner pilots and seven signed contracts. I graduated from NEXT AI as the company's technical co-founder.
